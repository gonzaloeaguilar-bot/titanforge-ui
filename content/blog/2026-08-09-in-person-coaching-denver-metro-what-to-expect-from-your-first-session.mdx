If you are booking your first in-person coaching session in the Denver Metro area, you might be wondering what actually happens when you walk through the door.

Most commercial gym trainers hand you a clip board, make you do 50 jumping jacks, and put you on a random leg press machine until you throw up.

That is not real coaching. That is high-priced amateur babysitting.

When you work with a professional evidence-based strength and longevity coach, your first session is a structured physical and bioenergetic diagnostic.

Here is the exact breakdown of what to expect during your initial consultation and movement assessment.

## Step 1: structural movement screening and mobility audit

Before laying a finger on a barbell or dumbbell, a real coach needs to understand how your joints move under load.

We audit ankle dorsiflexion, hip internal rotation, thoracic spine extension, and shoulder impingement risks. This involves both table-based passive range-of-motion assessments and active unloaded movement patterns. We measure ankle dorsiflexion using the weight-bearing lunge test to determine whether your squat depth is restricted by calf stiffness or structural hip anatomy. We assess scapular upward rotation and thoracic extension with wall-slide drills to verify whether overhead pressing is currently safe for your rotator cuff tendons.

If you sit at a desk in tech, finance, or executive management for 10 hours a day, your hip flexors are shortened and your glutes are functionally inhibited. When you transition directly from a chair to heavy axial loading, your pelvis tips anteriorly, dumping excessive shear forces onto your lower spine.

Loading a squat or overhead press without addressing movement compensation is a direct ticket to lumbar disc herniation or shoulder labrum tears (Cook et al., *Int J Sports Phys Ther*, 2014).

We screen movement quality first so every exercise in your custom program builds muscle safely without grinding down your joints.

## Step 2: body composition baseline and hypertrophy targets

Fatigue is not the metric of success. Adaptation is.

During your first session, we establish your structural baselines using multi-frequency bioelectrical impedance or calliper pinch data alongside tape measurements.

We evaluate lean muscle mass, visceral fat percentage, and basal metabolic rate (BMR).

Building lean muscle tissue is the single most powerful intervention for glucose regulation, insulin sensitivity, and long-term metabolic health (Schoenfeld et al., *J Strength Cond Res*, 2021).

If your goal is fat loss, increasing skeletal muscle mass expands your daily caloric ceiling so you can stay lean without eating like a rabbit.

## Step 3: nutrition, energy balance, and bioenergetic audit

Training only accounts for 3 to 4 hours of your week. What you do during the other 164 hours dictates 80% of your body composition results.

We audit your current daily caloric intake, protein distribution, hydration habits, and sleep architecture.

Most busy denver executives suffer from severe under-fueling during the workday followed by late-night overconsumption.

We build a flexible macro distribution strategy (1.8–2.2g of protein per kilogram of target body weight) to maximize muscle protein synthesis and control systemic hunger (Morton et al., *Br J Sports Med*, 2018).

## Step 4: setting progressive overload and autoregulation bounds

In your first session, we test your baseline strength across core movement patterns: hinge, squat, push, pull, and carry.

We don't take you to absolute muscular failure on day one. Instead, we determine your baseline Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) and Reps in Reserve (RIR). We run submaximal test sets in the 3 to 6 repetition range at an RPE of 6 or 7. This allows us to observe bar velocity, positional breakdown thresholds, and unilateral asymmetries without creating excessive systemic fatigue.

A scientific training program relies on mechanical tension and progressive overload over time (Westcott et al., *Curr Sports Med Rep*, 2012).

We establish a baseline logbook so every single week you know the exact weight, sets, and reps required to force neuromuscular adaptation. We define your initial minimum effective volume (MEV) for each muscle group, ensuring you do enough high-quality work to stimulate growth without exceeding your maximum recoverable volume (MRV).

We also set up autoregulation protocols so when work stress spikes or travel ramps up, your training scales dynamically without causing chronic adrenal fatigue.

## Step 5: executive lifestyle, stress management, and longevity integration

Stress is cumulative. Your central nervous system does not differentiate between an intense deadlift set and a stressful quarterly earnings call.

Chronic psychological stress elevates systemic cortisol, impairs recovery, and degrades sleep quality (Lennon et al., *Cell Metab*, 2021).

Your initial session outlines how we align your training schedule with your lifestyle constraints, travel demands, and recovery metrics. We establish clear boundaries between high-intensity resistance training and low-intensity recovery modalities.

We incorporate cardiovascular Zone 2 protocols to enhance mitochondrial density, improve capillary density, and optimize VO2 max for long-term cardiovascular health and longevity. We program these steady-state aerobic sessions at a precise conversational pace (typically 60–70% of maximum heart rate) on non-lifting days or post-workout to avoid blunting the molecular signaling pathways responsible for muscular hypertrophy.

Coaching isn't just about looking good in July; it's about being strong, mobile, and capable 30 years from today.

## FAQ

### Do I need to be in baseline shape before my first in-person session?

No. Showing up deconditioned or after a multi-year training layoff is common among the executives and professionals I work with. The diagnostic session is designed to establish where your joint tolerance, cardiovascular capacity, and movement mechanics sit right now. We tailor every movement progression to your current tissue capacity rather than expecting you to survive a pre-packaged workout.

### What if I have a history of chronic lower back pain or joint injuries?

We modify movement patterns around structural limitations rather than forcing standard barbell lifts. If a traditional back squat causes lumbar shear or hip impingement, we evaluate goblet squats, safety squat bar variations, or belt squats. The initial diagnostic identifies which movement planes cause discomfort so we can build strength around past injuries without aggravating irritated tissues.

### Where do in-person coaching sessions take place in the Denver Metro area?

Sessions are conducted in private, fully equipped training facilities in the south Denver metro area (including Parker, Lone Tree, Centennial, and DTC). This environment eliminates the distractions, equipment wait times, and chaos of commercial franchise gyms, allowing us to focus entirely on movement execution, logbook tracking, and physiological data collection.

### How sore will I be after the initial diagnostic session?

You should not experience debilitating delayed onset muscle soreness. Because our first session focuses on diagnostic screens, movement pattern testing, and submaximal RPE assessments rather than training to muscular failure, the mechanical damage is minimal. You will feel muscular engagement, but you will walk out fully functional for your workday.

### How often do I need to train in person versus completing sessions on my own?

Most clients work with me in person 1 to 2 times per week for heavy technical lifts, movement adjustments, and accountability checks, while completing 1 to 3 supplementary sessions independently using their customized logbook app. This hybrid model provides high-touch coaching on complex lifts while offering maximum scheduling flexibility for travel and executive work weeks.
