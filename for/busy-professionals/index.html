Fitness Coaching For Busy Professionals

Fitness coaching for adults whose training has to survive work, travel, family, imperfect weeks, and limited mental energy.

Updated October 13, 2026

Can Fitness Coaching Work With A Busy Schedule?

Yes, when the plan is built around the week you actually have. Titan Forge Fitness, founded and led by Steve "Doc" Ginevan, PhD, coaches clients in person at its Englewood, Colorado gym and online from anywhere. For people with work, travel and family commitments, the plan starts from how many sessions you can repeat, how long each one can be, and what equipment you will have on the road.

Standard Coaching is delivered through phone or pre-recorded Loom check-ins rather than scheduled video calls, so feedback does not depend on finding a free hour on both calendars. Premium Coaching adds scheduled video calls for people who want more direct contact. Either way, a week that goes badly is reported and the plan is adjusted, instead of starting over.

What Is The Least Training That Still Builds Strength?

Less than many people assume. A 2020 systematic review in Sports Medicine (Androulakis-Korakakis and colleagues, PMID 31797219) looked for the minimum training dose needed to raise one-repetition-maximum strength in resistance-trained men. All six studies that met its criteria found that a single set, performed one to three times a week, was enough to produce significant strength gains on the squat and bench press.

That is a floor, not a target. The review studied trained men and strength, not muscle size or fat loss, so it does not mean one set is ideal for everyone. It does mean a short session on a hard week still counts, which is why a Titan Forge plan defines a minimum session you can do when time runs out.

How Many Days A Week Should A Busy Person Lift?

Two well-planned days can cover the essentials. A 2016 meta-analysis in Sports Medicine (Schoenfeld and colleagues, PMID 27102172) compared training frequencies across ten studies with total volume kept equal. Training each muscle group twice a week produced greater muscle growth than training it once a week. Whether three times is better than two could not be settled from the available data.

In practice, two full-body sessions a week train every major muscle group twice. A third session, when the week allows, adds volume rather than rescuing the plan. What a two-session week usually looks like

Each session covers a squat or leg-press pattern, a hinge such as a deadlift or hip thrust, a press and a pull, with a few sets each. Protein and a simple meal default carry the nutrition side, and the check-in records what was done, what was missed and why. The exact exercises, loads and session length come from your assessment, not from this example.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is this page for?

Adults whose training needs to survive work, travel, family, and imperfect weeks without restarting every time life gets busy.

What changes for busy professionals?

The plan has to reduce decision fatigue, protect the most important habits, and adjust quickly when the week does not go perfectly.

What if my schedule changes every week?

The plan should define minimum effective actions, travel defaults, backup meals, and decision rules so one chaotic week does not become a full restart.

Is this only for executives?

No. Busy professional means the plan has to survive real obligations, not that the person needs a specific title or income level.

What is the first priority for busy clients?

Remove obstacles. The first win is usually a repeatable schedule, protein anchor, training minimum, and check-in rhythm that keeps the plan alive.
