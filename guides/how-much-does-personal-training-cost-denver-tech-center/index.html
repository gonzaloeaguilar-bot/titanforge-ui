How do you compare a monthly rate with a per-session rate?

Count the whole month, not the unit price. Add up what you would pay for the sessions you would actually attend, then ask what you get on the days between them. Two sessions a week leaves five days with no one in the room.

A monthly coaching rate usually pays for that part: a written plan, a check-in on a set day, and changes when the week goes wrong. Neither is better in general. In-person training is the better buy when the problem is technique, confidence in a gym, or needing a person beside you. Coaching is the better buy when the problem is the other five days.

What should you ask before you pay any trainer?

  1. What is the rate per session, and per package? Ask for both in writing. Packages usually lower the price of each session, and the JCC rate card shows it.
  2. What happens if you cancel or miss a session? The JCC card says its packages are non-transferable and non-refundable and that appointments need 24 hours’ notice. Rules like these are normal. The point is to know them before you pay.
  3. Who is the trainer, and what do they hold? Ask which certification they have and whether an independent body accredits it. The National Commission for Certifying Agencies publishes a directory of accredited certification programs you can check.
  4. What do I get between sessions? A plan you can follow alone, a way to ask questions, someone who reads your log. If the answer is nothing, treat the sessions as the whole service.
  5. What happens if it is not working? A good answer names a time frame and what would change, such as reviewing the plan.

Is online coaching cheaper than in-person personal training?

Per month it often is, but the two are priced on different units: the hour for a trainer, the month for coaching. Compare the total for the month and what each includes between sessions.

Is a more expensive trainer a better trainer?

Price tracks experience, credentials and attention, not outcome. The JCC prices three tiers by qualifications and years of service. No trainer controls your sleep or your week, and none can honestly guarantee a result.

Do session packages save money?

Usually they lower the price of each session. The catch is the terms: the JCC’s packages are non-refundable, so read the cancellation and expiry rules before you buy a large pack.
