Is Creatine Safe To Take?

For healthy adults, the research says yes. The International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand on creatine (Kreider and colleagues, 2017, Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, PMID 28615996) concluded that short- and long-term supplementation, studied at up to 30 grams a day for five years, was safe and well tolerated in healthy individuals and in a range of patient groups.

That does not make creatine essential: it supports hard training, it does not replace it. Anyone with kidney disease, anyone who takes medication or anyone under 18 should check with a qualified healthcare professional first. More detail: Creatine: what it does and does not do.

How Much Vitamin D Is Too Much?

More is not better with vitamin D. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements sets the tolerable upper intake level for adults at 100 micrograms, or 4,000 IU, a day, counting food, fortified products and every supplement together. Clinicians may use higher amounts for a diagnosed deficiency, but under supervision.

That is why Titan Forge's guidance starts with need: whether your diet, sun exposure or a blood test points to a gap, and what else you already take. Vitamin K, which is often paired with D3, can interact with warfarin and similar blood thinners, so anyone on those medicines should not start a K2 product without their prescriber. Frequently Asked Questions

Which supplement should come first?

Only consider a supplement after naming the gap. Protein powder may help intake convenience; creatine can support training performance.

Can supplements replace coaching?

No. They do not replace programming, nutrition decisions, accountability, or recovery.

Is creatine required?

No. Creatine is optional support, not a requirement for progress.

How should I judge supplement claims?

Be skeptical of claims that bypass training, nutrition, sleep, recovery, or medical context.

Who should ask a professional first?

Anyone with medical conditions, medications, pregnancy, kidney concerns, or uncertainty about safety.
