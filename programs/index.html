What Makes A Training Program Work?

Enough weekly work per muscle, increased over time. A 2017 meta-analysis in the Journal of Sports Sciences (Schoenfeld and colleagues, PMID 27433992) analyzed 15 studies and found a graded dose-response relationship between weekly training volume and muscle growth: each additional weekly set per muscle was associated with a 0.37 percent greater gain in muscle size.

That is why a good program counts sets per muscle across the week and adds load or sets as you adapt, which is what progressive overload means. More is not endlessly better: the extra work only helps if you can recover from it, and that limit is different for everyone. Read how many sets per muscle per week and what progressive overload really means.

How to read the 0.37 percent figure

The figure is an average slope across the studies in the analysis, not a promise that every added set adds the same amount for every person. Most of the included studies ran for weeks to a few months, and the analysis could not say exactly where the benefit levels off. Treat it as evidence that volume matters, then let your recovery decide how much of it you can use.

When should I choose coaching instead of a program?

Choose coaching when you need form feedback, nutrition changes, accountability, or help adjusting the plan when progress stalls.

Who can use a structured program?

A structured program can fit someone who already understands basic follow-through, can self-monitor recovery, and mainly needs a plan to follow rather than weekly decision support.

Can a program become coaching later?

Yes. A program can expose whether the missing piece is the plan itself or the feedback loop around the plan. If follow-through keeps breaking, coaching is the next step.
